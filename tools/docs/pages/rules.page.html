@xufa/rules

Business rules in workbooks of Excel, run as functions: decision tables, calculations in steps, constants, datatypes, transforms, and the tests that check them, written by the people who know the rules in the tool they know. Their expressions are @xufa/expression: safe, compiled once. Workbooks are read and written with no other dependency; books are kept in a database in versions, each tested before it is kept, and edited in the admin.

What it is for

  • Prices, rates and discounts by the values of a customer, a product, a date: decision tables that the business keeps.
  • Calculations in steps (a quote, a score, a recommendation) made of those tables.
  • Rules that change often: a new version is a workbook uploaded or edited in the admin, tested before it replaces the active one, and running in every process seconds later.

Books and tables

A book is every table of every sheet of a workbook. A table is a block of cells apart from the others by empty rows and columns, anywhere in a sheet; its first cell says what it is (Rules, Spreadsheet, Test...). Each table is a function of the book by its name (book.call(name, ...args), book.fn(name)), and every expression of the book sees the others, the constants of the book and Fun (helpers: Fun.mean, Fun.sort...).

Expressions

Cells can compute: a cell = expression of a table, the script of RET, the steps of a spreadsheet, conditions written as expressions, the body of a method, '@expression' in a transform. Expressions are those of @xufa/expression: JavaScript without statements or assignments (operators, ?., ??, ? :, template literals, arrays and objects, spreads, arrow functions, methods of values such as list.map(...) and text.slice(1)), compiled once and safe: they read and compute, nothing else. As in the engine this one follows, a member of null or undefined is undefined (no error), a trailing ; is dropped, and this is the result a spreadsheet builds.

Rules

Decision tables: the result is the cell of the row and column whose conditions the values meet.

Rules Float Premium(String plan, Integer age)    <- the type of the result, the name, the parameters
C1       | HC1       | RET                         <- C<n>: a column of conditions; HC<n>: a row of them; RET
plan     | age       |                             <- under RET: a script of the result (RET * 1.21)
String   | IntRange  | Float                       <- the types of the conditions, and of the result
         | <25       | [25..60)  | >=60
basic    | 120       | 100       | 150
premium  | 220       | 180       | 260
  • Conditions: a value (String, Integer, Float, Double: texts compared without accents), <, <=, >, >= and ranges [a..b], (a..b), [a..b) (IntRange, FloatRange, DoubleRange); an empty condition takes any value.
  • Results: the first cell that matches (undefined when none), as its type says (Integer, Float, Boolean, Date, String, Code, Codes: a list). A cell =expression is computed with the book's names and the parameters.
  • A book refuses a table with repeated combinations of conditions, saying where (RULES!A1: Duplicated combination at table Premium).

SmartRules and SimpleRules

The decision tables of OpenL Tablets that people write most: a row of titles, then a rule each row; the first rule whose conditions the arguments meet gives the result (undefined when none does).

  • SmartRules: each title is matched to a parameter, or a field of a datatype parameter (Driver Age is driver.age), by its words; the columns after the conditions are results. SimpleRules: the columns are the parameters, in their order.
  • Two columns under one merged title are a range: from (included) and to (excluded); either can be empty.
  • A column whose title says true (is true?) has conditions written as expressions; an empty cell is met.
  • A first column titled Rule names the rules: $Rule in their expressions, as $RuleId is the number of the rule.
  • Of several result columns, the first that is not empty is the result; a rule whose results are all empty goes on to the next rule. Results can be expressions (= history > 0 ? ... : null).

The language of OpenL

A book whose formulas are of OpenL (or whose tables only OpenL has) is a book of OpenL: book.dialect is 'openl' (or { dialect: 'openl' } as an option of RuleBook.fromSheets), and its expressions, cells and Method bodies are OpenL's language. They are parsed as OpenL and lowered to programs of @xufa/expression, which runs them as it runs every formula of JavaScript (the same checks, the same limit of steps); what OpenL does as Java are functions those programs call:

  • Rules are OpenL's decision tables: C1, HC1 (across), MC1, A1, RET1, CRET1 columns over rows of code, declared parameters (Double min, String[] plans, IntRange ages) and titles. A code that is only a parameter name is a simplified condition (plan with basic, gold); $C1.min is a cell of the rule, $Rule its name.
  • Conditions, Returns and Actions tables define elements (inputs, expression, parameters, title) that SmartRules use by their titles.
  • Java's numbers: int wraps, 10 / 4 is 2.5, long is a BigInt, BigDecimal a decimal of 34 digits, "x" + 1.0 is x1.0; nulls count as 0 in + and -; casts such as (byte) 200.
  • Arrays: items[select all having price > 50], select first, order by, split by, transform to; items.price projects a field; new Item[] { ... }, new Item(name = "Lamp").
  • Business phrases (a is less than b, age of the person), where bindings, and statements (variables, if, for, while, return; loops have a limit of steps).
  • About 150 functions of OpenL (sum, round(value, 2, HALF_UP), dateDif, textSplit, like...), ranges, dates and statics of Java such as Integer.MAX_VALUE. Books of JavaScript see these functions too.
  • Data records over rows (boxes.label, list:Type.field, references >customers), Test tables with _context_ columns, _res_.path and _error_.field, and versions chosen by fields of arguments (String usState : context) or called by their id; getContext(), modifyContext(name, value), setContext(context), emptyContext() and restoreContext() change the runtime context of the calls a rule makes.
  • SmartRules written in columns are read so; Data tables of lists of objects take a record over its rows (a blank first cell, or the same key again, goes on with it), and fields of List and Map types are arrays and Maps; a spreadsheet of a datatype gives an object of it.
  • Messages: msg(code, arguments...) gives a text of the messages option of the book (bundles by locale: { '': {...}, fr: {...}, de_DE: {...} }, which parseProperties reads of the .properties files of OpenL's i18n folders) in the locale of the runtime context, fr-CA taking fr_CA, then fr, then the default.
  • Values of LocalDate, LocalTime, LocalDateTime, ZonedDateTime and Instant of their ISO or US texts, written as Java writes them.

Each table can be in its own language: one book can have methods of OpenL and methods of JavaScript. A table's language is its property language (or dialect: openl or js, a row properties | language | js under its title); else that of a Properties table of its sheet (scope Worksheet), of its category (Category) or of the book (Module, which is then the language of the book); without them, the language its code is in when only one of them reads it (int total = 0; is OpenL, let total = 0 JavaScript); else the book's. Code that both read gives different results in each ("x" + 1.0, a null in a sum, 0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3), so a table that must keep its results says its language. book.table(name).dialect is the language of a table and languageFrom what told it (property, properties, code, book); the admin shows it by each table.

Each step is a value of the result and $name for the steps after it; +=, -=, *=, /=, %= work on its value; this.path (or .path) is a path in the result; return expression (without a name) is the result itself; delete $name takes a value out. A step that gives undefined adds a message to result.errors; a step that fails is left and told to onError.

Grids

A spreadsheet of more than one column of values is a grid, as in OpenL: each cell is $Column$Row, computed when it is read (in any order; a cell that reads itself through others is an error, and book.lint() finds it), $Row and $Column alone are the cells of its own column and row, $First:$Last the values between two rows (or columns), and Name : Type types a row or column. The result is an object of rows of columns; a spreadsheet of a type of one value (Double, String...) gives its RETURN row, or its last value. 

import { RuleBook } from '@xufa/rules';

const book = RuleBook.fromSheets([
  {
    name: 'RULES',
    rows: [
      ['Spreadsheet Double Budget(Double rent, Double food)', null, null],
      ['Step', 'Month', 'Year : Double'],
      ['Rent', '= rent', '= $Month * 12'],
      ['Food', '= food', '= $Month * 12'],
      ['Total', '= Fun.sum($Rent:$Food)', '= $Year$Rent + $Year$Food'],
      ['RETURN', null, '= $Total'],
    ],
  },
]);

console.log(book.call('Budget', 800, 300)); // a Double: its RETURN row
13200

Constants, datatypes, methods

Constants Rates            Datatype Customer        Method Float Round2(Float value)
Float   | vat  | 0.21      String  | plan | basic   return Math.round(value * 100) / 100
Integer | days | 30        Integer | age  |
  • Constants: typed values, names of every expression of the book.
  • Datatype: the fields of an input or a result: a type and a name each (their types convert the values of tests and Data tables), and a default value in a third column. The objects the book makes take the defaults of the fields they lack (empty, null or missing): Data records, results of SmartRules, the inputs of tests, and the arguments of calls of a datatype parameter (or a list of one: a copy of each object that lacks some takes them; the object of the application is not changed). Objects of datatypes in their fields are filled the same, at any depth and in lists; missing ones are not made. A default that is no value of its type is a message of book.test().
  • Method: a function whose body is an expression (return ...) or statements of JavaScript (variables, if, loops, functions), run as a program of @xufa/expression: as safe as every formula, with a limit of steps. Without a return, its value is that of its last statement.

Transform

An object made of another: each row is a path and a value. Texts '@expression' are computed (the input's fields by name, the context's names over them, the input as $), '@@...' is a text starting with @, and other texts are templates of the context ({{name}}, {{#items}}...{{/#}}: the block once for each item, the item its context, with _parent_ and _current_; a template of one {{ }} alone gives its value as it is). A path __apply__ is a list of { pipe: [names of functions], paths? }: the functions of the context, one after another, over the result (or over the values at its paths).

Properties and versions

A table's properties are rows properties | name | value under its title; a Properties table gives them to every table of its sheet (scope: Worksheet) or of the book (Module), and a table's own win. Tables of the same name whose properties say where each applies are versions of one table, and the runtime context of a call (book.withContext(context)) chooses one.

Only what the context tells is compared, and a table without a property applies to every value of it. Checks

A Check table names rules tables (and a route in their results) whose every possible value goes to a check function of the application: codes of products that must exist, limits that must hold. Its title is Check Name (the name of the function), and each row a table and, optionally, a route in its results (covers.*.code). 

const errors = await book.test({
  checks: {
    // Check Codes | Covers: every code the table Covers can give.
    Codes: async (values, tableName) => values.filter((code) => !known.has(code)).map((code) => `Unknown ${code} in ${tableName}`),
  },
});

Traces, gaps, checks and coverage

book.trace(name, args, context) gives the tree of the calls of a call: what each table got and gave, the version chosen and the cells of the sheet that gave each result. book.analyze(name) tries every case of the decision tables (conditions change only at their values and the ends of their ranges, so one value of each interval stands for all of it): the values of no rule (gaps, merged into ranges as conditions write them), those of several (overlaps: the first wins), the rules that never win, and the empty cells of lookups. book.testResults() tells how each row of each test went. book.lint() checks the formulas (expressions are lenient, so a typo is undefined, not an error): names that nothing defines, steps read before they are computed, members that Fun and the globals do not have, tables called with other arguments than they take, calls of what is no table, cycles of grids, and, in a book of both languages, tables that run in the book's language code that both read and compute differently (ambiguous-language: they should say their language; its fix is the row of the table that says it, and its sheetFix the rows that say it for the whole sheet, a Properties table of scope Worksheet, when that changes no other table of it). book.coverage() tells the tables and rules that no test reaches. book.testCase(name, args, { context }) writes a call as it goes now as a row of a Test table: its title (a test of a name no table has), the names of its columns (each argument, a column for each field of those that are objects, _context_ fields, _res_ or _res_.field, _error_ of a call that fails), their titles and its cells.

JSON

book.toJSON() is the book as data (the tables, compiled: className, params, matrix, lines...), and RuleBook.fromJSON(json) gives it back without reading a workbook.

Books in a database

RuleStore keeps books in a database of @xufa/orm (any backend), by name and tenant, in versions: each is tested before it is kept (refused when its tests fail, unless force), one is the active one, and the workbook it came from is kept as it came. Books are compiled once a version in each process; the active version is asked again after refresh ms (2000), so a new one reaches every process and machine in that time.

With tdz: true, a let or const in a Method of JavaScript that is used before its declaration runs is an error (rate is used before its declaration, with its line and column), as in JavaScript. By default it is undefined, so const total = base * rate; const rate = 1.21; gives NaN instead of failing. The option applies to every book of the store, also to books given to it already made (on a copy). Without a store, RuleBook takes the same option.

Books import books: a Method of JavaScript takes the tables of another book by its name (import { Discount, vat } from 'pricing': its rules, spreadsheets and methods as functions, its constants and data as values), and the store links the active version of it, of the same tenant, before the book runs, is tested or is inspected (a new active version is taken when the book is loaded again). Books importing each other, and books of no active version, are refused. store.dependents(name) gives the books that import one, and store.impact(name, { version }) runs their tests with another version of it (on copies): what making it the active one would break. With checkImporters: true such a version does not become the active one (save() and activate() refuse it, XUFA_RULES_ERR_BREAKS; 409 in the routes) unless force or breaking: true. 

import { RuleStore } from '@xufa/rules';

const rules = new RuleStore(db, { checks: { Codes: checkCodes } });
await rules.sync(); // its table (db.sync() and migrations make it too)

await rules.save('pricing', xlsxBytes, { author: 'ada', comment: 'new rates' }); // { version: 2, errors: [] }
await rules.call('pricing', 'Quote', customer);
const book = await rules.book('pricing', { tenant: 'acme', version: 1 });
await rules.activate('pricing', 1); // back to an older version

rulesPlugin gives a store routes of @xufa/http (app.rules is the store): GET / the books, GET /:name its versions and tables, GET /:name/xlsx and /:name/json (?version), POST /:name a new version (the bytes of a workbook or its JSON; 422 with the messages when its tests fail), POST /:name/test, POST /:name/run/:table ({ args }), PUT /:name/active and DELETE /:name.

The editor of the admin

With rules (a store), the admin has a page of books: workbooks uploaded as new versions, and each book edited as its sheets, a grid of cells as in Excel (arrows, Enter, Tab, typing, copy and paste with Excel itself, undo). Its tables are outlined by kind, the messages of its tests follow the changes, and saving makes a new version, tested first. Its tables are found by name, each with its properties (its own, and those of Properties tables apart) and, of versions, how many there are and a link to try one in the context that chooses it; each with its gaps, overlaps and rules that never win (marked on the grid); the rows of its tests are green or red; its tables can be tried with arguments and a runtime context, the cells that gave the result highlighted and the calls made shown as a tree, or measured (calls a second); new tables start from an example of each kind, and a rule for a gap is one click (its conditions written, the cursor at its result). The warnings of the formulas are with the messages, marked on their cells, and the rules no test reaches are marked too. The result of a try is a test with a click: a row added to the test of the table that has the same columns, or a new test under the cells of its sheet (a table no test runs links to a try of it). Its versions can be seen, compared with the one shown, their rows lined up as a diff does (rows inserted or deleted move none of the others): the cells that changed highlighted, or the two side by side (a gap opposite each row inserted or deleted), scrolled together, with the tables changed, new and gone), made the active one or downloaded. The permissions are _rules.view, _rules.change and _rules.delete; with tenants, the books are of the tenant chosen.

Safety

The expressions of cells read their context and compute: they cannot assign, reach prototypes, constructors, process or modules. The bodies of methods, of JavaScript or of OpenL, run on the same engine, as its programs: their variables are their own, they change only plain objects and arrays, and loops and calls have a limit of steps. A book is data, never code. A step whose expression is refused (input.constructor.constructor(...)) is left, and book.test() says where it is.

Books of other engines

The layouts of its tables, its JSON and the messages of its tests are those of the engine it was made after (itself made to replace OpenL Tablets), so books written for it load as they are, and give the same results: checked on real books, on every combination of the conditions of every table. What changes: texts are compared normalized on both sides; parameters are names of = cells and scripts; Multi tables may repeat combinations; a trailing ; of a formula is dropped and this is the result of a spreadsheet; methods of statements run as programs of @xufa/expression (no this, classes nor destructuring).

Performance

On a real book of 14 rules tables and a spreadsheet of 23 steps (Node.js 24), against the engine it was made after: its rules tables run 1.06 million times a second (6.7 times as many), the spreadsheet 32,600 times a second (5.8 times), and the workbook is read in 7 ms (exceljs: 23 ms). Conditions are compiled once, cells =expression the first time they run, and a call makes no objects until a cell needs its context.
